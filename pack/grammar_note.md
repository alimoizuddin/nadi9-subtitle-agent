# Nadi-9 — Grammar Note (field notes, P. Varma, Nov 2024)

These notes come from six weeks in the Nadi valley. They are incomplete. Where I was unsure
I have said so.

## G-ORDER-1 · Word order
The basic order is Subject – Object – Verb. The verb is the last content word of the clause.
Adverbs of time (kalu "tomorrow", ennal "today") usually sit between the subject and the verb.
A vocative (the name or kinship term of the person addressed) opens the sentence and is
followed by a pause, written as a comma.

## G-POST-1 · Postpositions
Nadi-9 uses postpositions, not prepositions. They follow the noun: *pura ke* "to the village",
*bus se* "by bus", *sanu pisu* "after a year".

## G-TENSE-1 · Past
Past tense is the suffix **-ri** on the verb root: *kona* "eat" → *konari* "ate".

## G-TENSE-2 · Future
Future tense is the suffix **-sa**: *ela* "come" → *elasa* "will come".

Present tense seems to be the bare root, but I recorded very few present-tense sentences and
would not rely on this.

## G-NEG-1 · Negation
The negative particle **mo** comes immediately before the verb: *ma mo konari* "I did not eat".
I never heard it after the verb.

## G-RESPECT-1 · Pronoun of address
Speakers address older relatives, elders and people of higher status with **tuvan**. Peers,
younger people and children are addressed with **tu**. Using *tu* to a parent is rude.

## G-RESPECT-2 · Respect suffix on the verb
When the subject of a verb is older than, or of higher status than, the speaker, the verb takes
**-ji** after the tense suffix: *tuvan veluriji* "you (resp.) came back". The order is always
tense first, then -ji. Verbs whose subject is the speaker never take -ji.

## G-Q-1 · Questions
Yes/no questions end with the particle **ka**. Intonation alone is not enough.

## G-LOAN-1 · Loanwords
English words for modern things (bus, phone, city, ticket) are used unchanged. I did not hear
them adapted.

## G-POSS-1 · Possession
Possessive pronouns add **-ko**: *mako* "my", *tuko* "your (fam.)", *voko* "his/her". I have
only elicited these in isolation, never in running speech.

## Not covered
- Imperatives. I heard bare roots used as commands to children, but did not check commands
  to elders.
- "Let us …" (hortative). Not recorded.
- Plurals. Numerals are followed by the bare noun; I found no plural marking.
- Idioms and humour. I did not collect any.
- Numerals are written as digits in all written Nadi-9 I saw.
